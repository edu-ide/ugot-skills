# Genre atlas: laws, not moods

Genres are grouped by **time law**, because that is the axis a stuck writer never
changes. Scan the family headings first: whichever family you have never used is
your cheapest escape from sameness.

Each entry gives: tempo band · density law · harmony law · form law · voice law ·
production law · **inviolable** (what its listeners will not forgive losing) ·
**invert** (the one law worth breaking while the rest stay intact) · **surface**
(the concrete words a generator responds to).

Descriptions are compressed and therefore lossy. Where a claim is contested or I
am summarizing a living scene, it is marked *(verify)*. Never put an artist name
in a generation prompt; use the surface words.

---

## Family A — Asymmetric timeline / key pattern

Time is organized by a fixed, unevenly spaced guide pattern that everything else
braids against. In West African and Afro-diasporic practice this is the bell
pattern or timeline; the standard 12-pulse bell places seven strokes across twelve
eighths. The Afro-Cuban clave is the same functional device. Nothing in this
family lines up neatly with a Western bar, and that is the point.

- **Salsa / Afro-Cuban son** — 180–220 BPM in cut time · density: clave-braided,
  tumbao bass anticipating the downbeat · harmony: functional diatonic with
  extended chords, montuno vamps · form: song head then extended montuno and
  mambo sections · voice: belted call-and-response with coro · production:
  live-room, horn-forward. **Inviolable:** the clave, and the bass never landing
  on beat 1. **Invert:** put the bass on the downbeat and let the piano carry
  anticipation. **Surface:** "montuno piano, tumbao bass, timbales, two-trumpet
  horn line, 1970s New York studio."
- **Highlife (Ghana)** — 100–130 BPM · density: gome timeline with palm-wine
  guitar arpeggios · harmony: cyclic diatonic loops · form: cyclic verses with
  long instrumental stretches · voice: relaxed group unison · production: warm,
  horn-and-guitar forward. **Inviolable:** the interlocking guitar figure.
  **Invert:** remove the guitar and let horns carry the timeline. **Surface:**
  "palm-wine guitar, gome rhythm, brass section, 1970s Accra."
- **Reggaeton / dembow** — 120–145 BPM · density: kick on the downbeat with
  syncopated snare or clap answering off the beat, busy offbeat hats · harmony:
  two- or four-chord minor loop · form: build with pre-chorus into hook · voice:
  half-sung MC · production: hyper-clean, sub-forward. **Inviolable:** the dembow
  itself. **Invert:** keep the dembow but strip it to hats only under the hook.
  **Surface:** "dembow pattern, tresillo bass, clap layer, wide sub."
- **Brazilian funk (funk carioca)** — 130–150 BPM · density: tamborzão-family
  percussion loop, heavily sampled *(verify the specific pattern before naming
  it)* · harmony: minimal or a single vamp · form: chant-and-loop · voice:
  shouted chant, group response · production: loud, mid-forward, raw.
  **Inviolable:** the percussion loop's identity as a sample. **Invert:** replace
  the sampled loop with a played acoustic ensemble at the same rhythm.
  **Surface:** "tamborzão loop, chanted vocal, distorted 808, Rio baile."
- **Brazilian phonk** — 130–160 BPM · fusion of funk carioca with drift-phonk
  cowbell and distortion *(scene in motion)* · harmony: single minor drone · form:
  loop plus drop · voice: chopped, processed · production: distortion-as-texture.
  **Inviolable:** the cowbell and the saturation. **Invert:** clean it entirely and
  keep only the pattern. **Surface:** "808 cowbell, saturated bass, funk
  percussion, chopped vocal."
- **Cumbia (and cumbia villera)** — 90–110 BPM · density: guacharaca scrape with
  offbeat accents · harmony: two-chord loops, minor-leaning · form: cyclic verses ·
  voice: plain narrative, often collective on the hook · production: synth-forward
  and cheap-by-choice in the villera branch. **Inviolable:** the scrape-and-offbeat
  lilt. **Invert:** keep the lilt and put a full stop on every second bar.
  **Surface:** "guacharaca scrape, cheap keyboard organ, accordion, timbal, Buenos
  Aires barrio."
- **Samba / partido-alto** — 95–105 BPM · density: surdo call on 2, pandeiro
  sixteenths, cuíca · harmony: rich diatonic with passing chords · form: cyclic
  with refrain · voice: group refrain against solo verse · production: live
  percussion ensemble. **Inviolable:** the surdo's accent on 2, not 1. **Invert:**
  move the surdo to 1 and watch how much of the genre it carries. **Surface:**
  "surdo, pandeiro, cavaquinho, cuíca, roda de samba."
- **Afrobeats** — 100–115 BPM · density: syncopated kick pattern with triplet-lean
  hats and log-adjacent bass · harmony: modal loops, minimal movement · form:
  loop-with-mutation, hook-first · voice: melodic, conversational, ad-lib heavy ·
  production: clean, wide, sub-forward. **Inviolable:** the relaxed
  behind-the-beat pocket. **Invert:** play everything exactly on the grid and let
  only the voice lag. **Surface:** "Lagos pocket, shaker, muted guitar plucks,
  soft 808, ad-libs."
- **Amapiano** — around 112 BPM · density: log drum as a wide percussive bass
  *punctuating* the groove rather than playing a bassline; shaker and rimshot
  sixteenths · harmony: jazzy minor loops, seventh and ninth chords · form: long
  cyclic build, no chorus in the pop sense · voice: sparse, often chanted or
  absent · production: space-as-content, deep sub. **Inviolable:** the log drum's
  role as punctuation. **Invert:** give the log drum a continuous melodic bassline
  and see the genre dissolve — instructive, and a real design option.
  **Surface:** "log drum, shaker, rimshot, jazzy Rhodes chords, wide sub, Pretoria."
- **Gqom** — 120–130 BPM · density: broken 3-step or 4-step patterns, kick clusters
  rather than a steady four · harmony: near-absent, one dark motif · form: loop and
  tension, minimal release · voice: chanted fragments · production: raw, mono-forward.
  **Inviolable:** the missing steady kick. **Invert:** restore a four-on-the-floor
  under the same top layer. **Surface:** "broken 3-step kick, dark motif, chanted
  fragment, Durban."
- **Afro house** — 118–126 BPM · density: four-on-the-floor with layered African
  percussion · harmony: modal vamps · form: build–drop with long intros · voice:
  chanted or wordless · production: wide, deep. Fastest-growing sound of 2025 by
  sample-download share *(one platform's data)*. **Inviolable:** percussion layering
  density. **Invert:** strip to kick and voice only.

**Never used this family?** That is likely your single biggest available jump.


### Lesser-known, high-rhythm entries

These are rarely heard outside their scenes, and each is defined by a law rather
than a palette — which is what makes them usable rather than decorative.

- **Shangaan electro (Limpopo / Soweto)** — 180–190 BPM · density: complex fast
  patterns entirely in the mids and highs · harmony: marimba-like synth riffs as
  harmony · form: relentless loop, no build · voice: sped-up vocal samples and
  group chant · production: **no bass line and no heavy kick drum**, lightness by
  subtraction. **Inviolable:** the missing low end. **Invert:** add a sub and a
  four-on-the-floor kick — the euphoria dies immediately, which proves where it
  lived. **Surface:** "180 BPM marimba synth patterns, no bass, no kick drum,
  sped-up group vocals, hand-clap layer." Genuinely radical for a generator: it
  is defined by what is absent, and absence is the one thing a generator obeys
  well.
- **Funaná (Santiago, Cape Verde)** — 140–160 BPM in 2/4 · density: unrelenting
  syncopated scrape on the ferrinho, an iron bar scraped with a knife · harmony:
  simple fast progressions under a diatonic accordion (gaita) · form: cyclic
  dance form · voice: plain Creole narration with social commentary ·
  production: raw, live, mid-forward. **Inviolable:** the continuous scrape.
  **Invert:** silence the scrape for one whole section. **Surface:** "diatonic
  accordion lead, iron bar scraped with a knife on every offbeat, fast two-four,
  raw live band."
- **Mbalax (Senegal)** — fast, danceable · density: polyrhythmic sabar drum
  ensemble with a tama talking drum answering conversationally · harmony:
  repetitive progressions with syncopated guitar riffs · form: song form opening
  into drum-led sections · voice: Wolof/Serer vocal style, declamatory ·
  production: percussion-forward band. **Inviolable:** the sabar ensemble's
  interlocking density. **Invert:** reduce to one drum and let the guitar carry
  the polyrhythm. **Surface:** "sabar drum ensemble, talking drum answering the
  singer, syncopated electric guitar riff, Dakar band."
- **Dangdut koplo (East Java)** — 110–140 BPM · density: rapid rolling kendang
  patterns with frequent double-time fills and breaks · harmony: minor-leaning
  loops with synth and electric guitar · form: verse–chorus with crowd-rousing
  breaks · voice: ornamented melodic lead · production: loud, synth-forward.
  **Inviolable:** the rolling kendang and its double-time fill. **Invert:** keep
  the kendang pattern and remove every fill.

## Family B — Additive / aksak cells

Time is built from unequal cells alternating binary and ternary quantities —
2+3, 2+2+3, 2+3+3 — reiterated fast. The name means "limping." Western ears hear
it as a permanent stumble that never resolves, which is exactly why it is
unforgettable.

- **Balkan folk-derived (Bulgarian, Macedonian, Greek)** — fast · density: aksak
  cells, 7/8 as 3+2+2 or 2+2+3, 9/8 as 2+2+2+3 · harmony: modal, drone-leaning,
  parallel seconds in choral practice · form: cyclic dance forms · voice: open
  chest voice, tight non-tempered harmony · production: dry, close, unblended.
  **Inviolable:** the unequal cell. **Invert:** keep the melody and set it in 4/4 —
  it becomes generic instantly, proving where the identity lives. **Surface:**
  "7/8 as three plus two plus two, kaval, tapan, open-throated women's harmony."
- **Chalga / manele / Turkish arabesk** *(verify per-scene detail)* — mid-fast ·
  density: aksak or dembow-adjacent hybrids · harmony: harmonic-minor colored,
  augmented-second inflections · form: verse–chorus imported over a non-Western
  time law · voice: heavy ornamentation · production: synth-forward, loud.
  **Inviolable:** the ornament. **Invert:** sing it perfectly straight.
- **Songs in 5 or 7 without the folk surface** — the law alone, on a modern
  palette. **Surface:** "verses in five, chorus in seven, no fill across the
  change, electric bass and drum kit only."

## Family C — Nested cycles and long-form cyclic time

Time is a cycle marked by nested subdivisions, not a bar count. Sections are
positions in the cycle rather than blocks in a sequence.

- **Gamelan (Javanese)** — slow to moderate · density: colotomic nesting, where
  specific gongs mark nested intervals (kempyang, ketuk, kempul, kenong, gong) ·
  harmony: slendro/pelog tuning systems, not equal temperament · form: the gong
  cycle *is* the form · voice: sindhèn floating above the cycle · production:
  room-as-instrument. **Inviolable:** the gong marking the cycle's end.
  **Invert:** withhold the closing gong once. **Surface:** "nested gong cycle,
  bonang interlocking, wooden mallets, long decay." Using the tuning system in a
  generator is unreliable; the *cycle* transfers, the tuning usually does not.
- **Hindustani / Carnatic classical** — variable · density: tala cycle grouped
  into unequal sections, tabla or mridangam marking the cycle · harmony: raga as
  melodic system over a tonic drone, no functional progression · form: slow
  exposition to fast development, cyclic return to the sam · voice: melismatic,
  microtonal ornament · production: dry, drone-forward. **Inviolable:** the drone
  and the return to the cycle's first beat. **Invert:** remove the drone and add
  one chord change. **Surface:** "tanpura drone, tabla cycle of sixteen, slow
  exposition, gradual acceleration."
- **Gnawa (Morocco)** — moderate, accelerating · density: interplay of triple and
  duple meter, iron qraqab castanets, three-string guembri/hajhuj · harmony:
  pentatonic riff-as-harmony, no chords · form: long trance suite, tempo rising ·
  voice: call-and-response, leader and chorus · production: dense hypnotic texture,
  designed to induce trance. **Inviolable:** the triple-duple ambiguity and the
  acceleration. **Invert:** fix the tempo absolutely and resolve the meter.
  **Surface:** "guembri bass riff, iron qraqab, call and response, accelerating
  trance suite."
- **Qawwali** — moderate, accelerating · density: handclaps and tabla driving a
  cycle · harmony: modal over drone, harmonium · form: expanding repetition of a
  single line · voice: soloist plus responding chorus, extreme melisma ·
  production: live ensemble, room. **Inviolable:** the escalating repetition.
  **Invert:** state the line once and never repeat it.

## Family D — Isochronous grid

An evenly spaced pulse with the accent architecture on top. This family contains
nearly all Western pop, which is why it is where you get stuck. Within it, the
distinctions live in **density** and **production**, so specify those or you will
get the family's average.

- **Soul / Motown** — 100–130 BPM · backbeat on 2 and 4, tambourine sixteenths ·
  functional diatonic with clear cadences · verse–chorus with a lift · belted,
  group answer · mono-forward, compressed. **Invert:** delete the backbeat and keep
  the tambourine.
- **Country (traditional)** — 90–130 BPM · train beat or two-beat bass · I-IV-V
  with pedal steel voice-leading · verse–chorus with a story turn · plain diction,
  audible consonants · dry, forward vocal. **Invert:** keep the diction and remove
  all cadential resolution.
- **City pop** — 95–120 BPM · sixteenth-note funk with jazz-fusion chord density ·
  extended chords, frequent modulation and passing dominants · long-form
  verse–chorus with instrumental breaks · smooth, unstrained · hyper-clean,
  wide, expensive-sounding. **Inviolable:** chord density plus surface calm.
  **Invert:** keep the chord density and record it filthy and close. **Surface:**
  "1980s Tokyo studio, slap bass, DX-style electric piano, chorused guitar, sax
  break, gated snare."
- **Disco / house** — 115–128 BPM · four-on-the-floor, offbeat open hat, octave
  bass · vamps and modal loops · build–drop or long cyclic · diva belt, or wordless ·
  wide, reverberant. **Invert:** remove the offbeat hat — the genre's actual spine.
- **Techno / hard techno / melodic techno** — 125–150+ BPM · relentless kick,
  minimal syncopation · static or single-motif harmony · long linear build, no
  chorus · voice absent or as texture · production is the composition. In 2026 the
  "Afterlife-style" emotionally expansive melodic techno is a dominant reference
  point, and hard techno reached mainstream scale without softening *(scene
  reporting)*. **Invert:** stop the kick for a whole section and keep the tension.
- **Drum and bass / jungle** — 165–180 BPM · breakbeat sixteenths over a
  half-tempo bassline · modal, minimal · intro–drop–switch · MC or wordless ·
  sub-forward. **Inviolable:** the tempo split between drums and bass.
  **Invert:** make the bass move at drum speed.
- **Footwork** — around 160 BPM · triplet-against-duple kick clusters, extreme
  repetition of a chopped vocal fragment · single sample as harmony · loop and
  mutation · vocal as percussion · raw. **Inviolable:** the stuttered fragment.
- **Jersey club** — 130–140 BPM · kick clusters in a five-stroke figure, chopped
  vocal, bed-squeak sample *(verify pattern before naming it)* · minimal harmony ·
  chant loop · shouted chant · loud, dry. **Invert:** keep the figure and slow it
  to half tempo.
- **Drift phonk** — 130–160 BPM · 808 cowbell melody, heavily distorted low end ·
  single minor motif · loop and drop · pitched-down chopped vocal ·
  distortion-as-texture. **Inviolable:** cowbell plus saturation. **Invert:** play
  the cowbell figure on a clean marimba.
- **PluggnB** — 120–150 BPM, half-time feel · sparse plugg drums, wide detuned
  bell synths · dreamy modal loops · loop and mutation · melodic auto-tuned R&B
  singing · lo-fi-clean hybrid. Combines plugg with 1990s R&B *(living scene)*.
  **Invert:** replace the auto-tune with a dry unprocessed voice.
- **Singeli** — 180–300 BPM · hyper-fast programmed drums · minimal harmony ·
  MC-driven cyclic form · shouted Swahili MC · raw, loud. **Inviolable:** the
  tempo. **Invert:** keep every element and halve the tempo.
- **Shoegaze / post-punk** — 100–140 BPM · steady pulse buried under guitar mass
  (shoegaze) or exposed by a driving eighth-note bass (post-punk) · modal, planing
  chords, riff-as-harmony · verse–chorus without a lift · voice mixed as one
  instrument among many, or declaimed flat · distortion or space as the content.
  **Inviolable:** the vocal's refusal to sit in front. **Invert:** put the voice
  dry and close in front of the wall.

## Family E — Free, rubato, and breath-clocked time

There is no grid. Duration is decided by breath, speech, or gesture. This family
is nearly absent from generator output, which makes it valuable and hard.

- **Enka / kayōkyoku ballad** — slow to moderate, rubato at phrase ends ·
  density: sparse, rhythm yielding to the singer · harmony: Western functional
  harmony in minor, with Japanese scalar color · form: verse–chorus with a long
  held climax · voice: heavy melisma (*kobushi*) and portamento, tightly
  controlled · production: strings and koto or shamisen with a forward vocal.
  **Inviolable:** the ornament on the held note. **Invert:** sing the climax
  perfectly straight with no vibrato. **Surface:** "shamisen and koto over
  Western strings, rubato phrase endings, heavy portamento, held final note."
- **Sean-nós, pansori, and other unaccompanied narrative song** — free ·
  no accompaniment, so the voice sets all six laws · **Invert:** add one
  isochronous instrument and hear the genre disappear.
- **Ambient / drone** — pulseless · density: change measured in tens of seconds ·
  harmony: static, one sonority · form: through-composed or endless · voice
  absent or wordless · space-as-content. **Inviolable:** the absence of event.
  **Invert:** insert one abrupt event and let it never recur.
- **Post-rock** — slow-building · pulse present but sections defined by dynamics ·
  modal, planing · long crescendo form, no chorus · voice absent or buried ·
  space-as-content. **Invert:** put the loudest event at the beginning.

## Family F — Speech-clocked time

The words set the rhythm; the beat accommodates them.

- **Drill** — 138–145 BPM in half-time feel · density: sliding 808 glides, sparse
  hats · harmony: single dark motif · form: loop with verse blocks · voice:
  conversational, behind the beat · production: dark, sub-forward.
  **Inviolable:** the glide. **Invert:** make the 808 stationary and let the hats
  glide instead.
- **Dembow / MC-led club** — see Family A; the MC's phrasing dictates edits.
- **Deconstructed club** — variable, often gridless · density: collage of club
  signifiers with the dance floor removed · harmony: none or sampled · form:
  abrupt juxtaposition · voice: processed fragment · production: hyper-artificial.
  **Inviolable:** the refusal to sustain a groove. **Invert:** sustain one groove
  for the whole track.
- **Talking blues, sprechgesang, recitative** — the voice is metrically free while
  the accompaniment keeps time. Cheap, effective, rare in generator output.

## Family G — Production-law-defined genres

Here the constitutive law is not in the notes at all. The mixing desk is the
instrument, which is a genuinely different way to compose.

- **Dub** — the desk is played in real time: parts muted and unmuted, fragments
  thrown into spring reverb and tape delay, the drum-and-bass riddim exposed. The
  *version* is a composition, and a riddim is a reusable object that many songs
  inhabit. **Inviolable:** the drop-out. **Invert:** never mute anything and let
  the effects do all the work. **Surface:** "riddim exposed, spring reverb throws,
  tape delay on the snare, bass and drums only for eight bars, 1970s Kingston
  desk."
- **Vaporwave / lo-fi** — the artifact is the content: sample-rate reduction, tape
  wobble, pitch drift. **Invert:** the same material rendered pristine — usually
  reveals the material was thin, which is a useful diagnosis.
- **Hyperpop** — extreme pitch-shifting, clipping-as-instrument, sudden formal
  cuts. **Invert:** one continuous unprocessed take.

## How to use this atlas against sameness

1. Write your last outputs' six law values. Find the family heading you have
   never opened.
2. Pick one genre from it and adopt all six laws, including the uncomfortable one.
   Do not translate it into your habitual palette — that is how the escape fails.
3. Or keep your current genre and invert exactly the element listed as
   **inviolable**. That is the one change that cannot be mistaken for a variation.
4. Run the distance script before you generate.
5. Name what you lose. Every law you adopt forecloses something; a genre choice
   that costs nothing was not a choice.
